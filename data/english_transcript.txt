2. The entire nation must work more to produce and for the country's activity as a whole to be more important throughout the year and for France's situation to improve.
3. I therefore propose that two public holidays be abolished for the entire country.
4. And it is a proposal rejected by more and more French people.
5. Last July 15, Prime Minister François Bairou unveiled his plan to restore public finances by proposing the abolition of two public holidays, Easter Monday and May 8.
6. A measure aimed at bringing in 4.2 billion euros out of the 44 billion euros targeted for the 2026 budget.
7. Just after these statements, 73% of French people were opposed to this abolition according to a survey by Ifop for Le Figaro.
8. This Monday, August 25, a new survey by Ifop was published by our colleagues at Le Parisien on the same subject and the finding is clear.
9. 84% of people surveyed are opposed to it.
10. An 11-point change in just one month which illustrates the almost total rejection by the French of such a measure.
11. For 80% of respondents, this abolition of public holidays is a disguised tax and 66% do not see the connection between working more and improving France's debt and deficits.
12. A clear message to the Prime Minister while the call to strike on September 10 is worrying the government.
